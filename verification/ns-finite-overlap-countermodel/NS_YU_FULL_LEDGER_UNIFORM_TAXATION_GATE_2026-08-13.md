# Navier–Stokes full-sequence ledger audit and uniform-taxation gate

**Date:** 2026-08-12 America/Denver / 2026-08-13 UTC  
**Scope:** hostile audit and synthesis of arXiv:2606.13887v1 with the threshold/depletion branch. This is not a regularity proof or blowup construction.

## Primary source

Runlong Yu, *Critical Ledgers and Scale-Defect Cascades for Navier–Stokes*, arXiv:2606.13887v1, submitted 2026-06-11.

The source is explicit that its unconditional theorem is finite-scale and that uniform taxation, negligible leakage, and local-to-global control remain open.

## What survives the audit

For an admissible nested parabolic chain `Q_{k+1} subset Q_k`, the source uses the standard critical quantities

\[
A_k=r_k^{-1}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_t\int_{B_{r_k}}|u|^2,
\quad
E_k=r_k^{-1}\iint_{Q_k}|\nabla u|^2,
\]

\[
C_k=r_k^{-2}\iint_{Q_k}|u|^3,
\quad
D_k=r_k^{-2}\iint_{Q_k}|p-(p)_{B_{r_k}}|^{3/2},
\]

and reservoir badness

\[
B_k=A_k+C_k+D_k.
\]

Its transition coordinates are absolute-value cutoff quantities:

\[
\Phi_k=r_k^{-1}\iint |u|^2|u\cdot\nabla\phi_k|,
\]

\[
\Pi_k=r_k^{-1}\iint |p-(p)_{B_{r_k}}||u\cdot\nabla\phi_k|,
\]

\[
\Lambda_k=r_k^{-1}\iint |u|^2(|\partial_t\phi_k|+|\Delta\phi_k|).
\]

The local energy inequality, local interpolation, and standard pressure decay give a valid one-step inequality of the form

\[
B_{k+1}-(1-\lambda)B_k
\le
\operatorname{Sup}^{\rm full}_k
-
\operatorname{Tax}^{\rm full}_k
+
\operatorname{Leak}^{\rm full}_k.
\]

The finite summation theorem then yields: if `B_k >= epsilon` for `0 <= k < N`,

\[
\sum_{k<N}
\bigl(\operatorname{Sup}^{\rm full}_k-
\operatorname{Tax}^{\rm full}_k\bigr)_+
\ge
\lambda\varepsilon N-B_0-
\sum_{k<N}\operatorname{Leak}^{\rm full}_k.
\]

This is real proof leverage. It avoids first-threshold attainment, packet-cover cardinality, and cross-scale Vitali arguments on a full nested dyadic orbit. Persistent CKN-critical badness must repeatedly generate untaxed supply or pay through non-negligible leakage.

## What the theorem does not prove

The inequality is a **survival alternative**, not a depletion inequality.

The supply terms are positive upper-bound quantities generated by nonlinear flux, pressure transport, interpolation amplification, and pressure regeneration. The tax contains dissipation and expected decay. The theorem does not prove

\[
\operatorname{Sup}^{\rm full}_k-
\operatorname{Tax}^{\rm full}_k
\le
\operatorname{Leak}^{\rm full}_k+\operatorname{Err}_k,
\]

nor does it prove that the positive part of untaxed supply consumes a separate finite global budget.

The closure estimates

\[
\Lambda_k\lesssim A_k,
\qquad
\Phi_k\lesssim C_k,
\qquad
\Pi_k\lesssim C_k^{1/3}D_k^{2/3}
\]

show only that transition coordinates are controlled by reservoir coordinates at the same scale. They do not make leakage summable and do not tax supply.

Consequently, the full-sequence ledger cannot be inserted into the Lean depletion theorem by renaming variables. The sign is wrong for that use: Yu proves that persistent badness **forces** cumulative untaxed supply, while the desired regularity bridge must show that visible supply **depletes** a finite budget or is uniformly taxed.

## Clean finite-window anti-phantom theorem: exact scope

The source also proves a clean finite-window positive-cone anti-kernel statement under an explicit energy-separation assumption. On a fixed periodic active window, positivity of the Reynolds covariance and nonnegative energy tests imply that a defect invisible to the positive energy channel is zero. Compactness then gives a finite coercivity constant `C_{Lambda,+}`.

This does not yet exclude the local Navier–Stokes cascade because:

1. `C_{Lambda,+}` depends on the active window; no scale-uniform lower bound is proved as the window grows or moves;
2. the local-to-clean chart and quotient comparison are assumptions;
3. pressure-tail, cutoff, truncation, nonlinear, reproduction, gauge, and profile errors remain PDE inputs;
4. the positive-cone theorem does not cover all sign-changing formal stress residuals;
5. finite-window injectivity does not imply a complete infinite-scale gap.

Thus the clean theorem identifies the right finite-dimensional firewall but leaves the two dangerous arrows explicit:

\[
\text{fixed window}\longrightarrow\text{uniform moving windows},
\]

\[
\text{clean periodic package}\longrightarrow\text{localized NS-realizable package}.
\]

## Corrected highest-leverage gate

The full-sequence route and the sparse-extraction route now have different exact gates.

### Lane A: full dyadic orbit

Prove

\[
\boxed{\texttt{NS-UNIFORM-TAXATION-OR-LEAKAGE-VANISHING}}
\]

in a form strong enough that every sustained bad orbit satisfies

\[
\frac1N\sum_{k<N}
\bigl(\operatorname{Sup}^{\rm full}_k-
\operatorname{Tax}^{\rm full}_k\bigr)_+
\longrightarrow0,
\]

and

\[
\frac1N\sum_{k<N}\operatorname{Leak}^{\rm full}_k
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Yu's finite survival theorem would then contradict `B_k >= epsilon` at every sufficiently small scale.

A more structural equivalent is: every NS-realizable profitable supply direction is quantitatively detected and charged, with a coercivity constant and localization error uniform along the orbit.

### Lane B: sparse or threshold-selected orbit

Prove the selected-interval depletion theorem banked in the companion note:

\[
\mathcal B_k-\mathcal B_{k+1}
\ge
c\lambda_{n_k}O_{n_k}(\widehat d_{n_k})^q-e_k,
\qquad
\sum e_k<\infty,
\]

on the exact extraction set, together with a divergent observability-weight series. The finite Lean telescope then forbids persistent visible events.

### Common final gate

Both lanes reduce to the same survivor:

\[
\boxed{\texttt{NS-COMBINED-INVISIBLE-PROFITABLE-CASCADE-EXCLUSION}.}
\]

No nonzero scale-critical package extracted from a suitable weak solution can remain simultaneously:

- Navier–Stokes realizable;
- profitable after dissipation and decay taxes;
- reproducible across scales;
- compatible with local pressure and local energy identities;
- invisible to pressure, flux, positive-energy, trace, and moving-window observations;
- outside the CKN decay basin at every scale.

This is the current minimum-cut theorem. It is stronger and more precise than the earlier request for a same-scale scalar absorption constant.

## Complexity–tax subgate

A promising quantitative split is to charge growth of the active finite window to dissipation.

For a normalized velocity package and a Fourier cutoff `P_{<=N}`, the elementary tail estimate is

\[
\|(I-P_{\le N})U\|_{L^2}^2
\lesssim
N^{-2}\|\nabla U\|_{L^2}^2.
\]

Therefore either a moderate window captures a fixed amount of the package, allowing a finite-window observability estimate, or the normalized enstrophy is large. Enstrophy already appears in Yu's tax. A successful theorem must make this dichotomy compatible with the pressure/covariance coordinates and with the moving-window local-to-clean errors.

The target is

\[
\boxed{\texttt{NS-WINDOW-COMPLEXITY-CHARGED-BY-TAX}.}
\]

It would say that failure of a window of size `N_k` to capture the profitable package forces a tax increment large enough to offset that scale's supply, while successful capture gives an observation lower bound whose degradation is slow enough for the depletion series to diverge.

This is a research target, not a theorem claimed here.

## Verdict on the original threshold architecture

The source audit does not rehabilitate the printed Shahmurov typed-zero endpoint. It provides a better bypass:

\[
\text{no CKN scale}
\Longrightarrow
\text{untaxed supply or leakage on a full dyadic orbit}
\Longrightarrow
\text{uniform taxation/detection problem}.
\]

This avoids relying on the audited ledger-extension, parent-seed, score/mass, first-threshold, and cross-scale-overlap implications. The fixed-threshold route remains only conditionally repairable; the full-sequence supply–tax route now has higher proof leverage.
