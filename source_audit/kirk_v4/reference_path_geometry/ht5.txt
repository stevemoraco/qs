Lemma 6.37 (Carrier–activity hypergraph coverage and no-reuse allocation). For a connected family
(g, F) form the finite incidence hypergraph Hg,F whose ordinary edges are the physical carrier segments and
whose hyperedge A is incident to the selected pivots IP (A). Root the carrier skeleton by the deterministic
order of Lemma 6.34. Then:
    (i) cutting every carrier at U (F) ∪ Rg (F) leaves an acyclic carrier graph;
   (ii) the depth-first first-incidence rule assigns every activity hyperedge A exactly once, at the least
        incident pivot first reached from the root; its remaining n(A) − 1 incidences are recorded only as
        possible side branches;
  (iii) the three payments are disjoint: the product Fourier–Wiener norm pays the tensor-product nuclear
        cost on U (F), the scalar coverage reserve pays the lost clean-pivot contractions, and the promoted
        magnitude wA,k(A) is inserted exactly once in the rooted spatial recursion.
Consequently no same-edge or cross-edge activity, shared carrier tail, closure edge, or cycle-breaking pivot
is charged twice in (90) or in the recursion (94).
Proof. By definition, every carrier cycle meeting an activity is cut at all of that activity’s incident pivots,
and every remaining activity-free carrier cycle is cut at its least closure pivot in Rg (F). Hence a cycle in
the residual carrier graph would avoid both sets, contradicting (87); this proves (i). On the resulting
rooted forest, deterministic depth-first exploration gives a total order on first visits to pivots. Assign a
hyperedge at the first visited member of its incidence set. This assignment is unique, while every other
incidence can only attach a branch to an already assigned hyperedge, proving (ii). The product-group
convolution of Lemma 6.35 contains the complete activity factor and all covered one-pivot densities before
any scalar split. Lemma 6.31 then separates its nuclear loss from the unused scalar incidence reserve.
The first-incidence exploration retains one and only one copy of the promoted activity magnitude. These
operations act on distinct factors of the majorant, proving (iii) and the final claim.
